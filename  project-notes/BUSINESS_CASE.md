## 1. The idea
Anti-Walk is a ride-sharing service designed to improve transportation accessibility in Antigonish by connecting local riders with drivers through a convenient, easy-to-use app. The idea behind Anti-Walk is to provide Antigonish with a more accessible and convenient transportation option. Current available transportation alternatives, including public transit and taxi services, often lack the flexibility and convenience that residents and students need. Anti-Walk aims to fill this gap by offering an on-demand service that allows users to request rides whenever and wherever they need them. Drivers can sign up through the app and complete a screening process before being approved to provide rides. Riders simply request a trip, receive an upfront price, and wait for a driver to accept their request. Unlike traditional taxi services, Anti-Walk offers transparent pricing rather than relying on a running meter. The service creates value for everyone involved: riders gain convenience and reliability, drivers receive an opportunity to earn additional income with flexible hours, the community benefits from improved transportation accessibility and efficiency, and ANTI-Walk (Me) collects a service fee. Anti-Walk aims to provide Antigonish with a more flexible transportation alternative that addresses the main problems associated with the current options available. 

## 2. Where IT creates value

One area that IT creates value for Anti-Walk is operations. The software allows riders to request transportation directly through the app, where available drivers can accept requests. This makes the process faster and more convenient than relying on traditional taxi services or scheduled public transit. Features like live GPS tracking also allow customers to see where their driver is and when they can expect them to arrive. This improves the overall customer experience while making the service more efficient.

Another area that IT creates value is marketing and sales. Having an app makes Anti-Walk more accessible to potential customers, particularly StFX students who are already familiar with existing rideshare services like uber. The software also allows me to collect useful information, such as popular pickup locations, busiest hours, and customer demand. This information can help me make better business decisions, such as determining where more drivers are needed and when demand is highest. I could also use this information to target promotions toward specific customer groups. 

## 3. Network effects

Anti-Walk has a two-sided network effect because it relies on both riders and drivers. The service becomes more valuable to riders as more drivers join because there are more available rides, shorter wait times, and better availability. On the other hand, more riders make the platform more valuable to drivers because there are more potential customers and opportunities to earn money. This creates a positive feedback loop where growth on one side of the platform provokes growth on the other. For example, if Anti-Walk has a large number of active drivers, customers are more likely to use the service because they know a ride is more likely to be available. As the number of customers increases, drivers have more opportunities to earn additional income, making them more likely to continue using the platform. 

The biggest challenge when starting Anti-Walk is the cold-start problem. Without drivers, riders have no reason to use the service, and without riders, drivers have little incentive to sign up. To overcome this, Id focus on recruiting drivers from the Antigonish community and the StFX student population before heavily promoting the service to customers. After finding drivers I could then promote the service through StFX, the student union, and local partnerships to attract riders. I might also consider offering discounts or promotions to encourage people to try the service. Building a small customer base would help kickstart the startup and give it some much needed exposure to both riders and drivers.

The dashboard page of my prototype demonstrates network effects by displaying the number of active drivers and registered riders. This visually demonstrates how the platform is growing as more people begin using the service. It also helps show how the growth of one group can benefit the other.

## 4. Can it be copied?

The Anti-Walk website itself would not be a major competitive advantage because anyone can easily copy it. The real advantage would come from the resources and capabilities I would develop in Antigonish. Here are a few things that make Anti-Walk harder for other companies to compete with me.
The first and most important resource would be a dependable network of local drivers. I would build long-term relationships with drivers by communicating with them regularly, listening to their concerns, and making sure the service works for them; once I have this strong relationship it would be very difficult for another company to swoop in and take them from me. I would also try to create an environment where drivers feel valued rather than easily replaceable. This is important because reliable drivers improve the customer experience. It could become rare if Anti-Walk (me) develops a strong group of drivers who consistently choose to work through the platform. Imitating would be challenging because a competitor cannot instantly recreate the trust and loyalty I built over time. Other companies could offer higher pay, but that may not be enough to replace established working relationships.

A second advantage would be my knowledge of the local transportation market. Since I go to school in Antigonish, I have knowledge of which areas have the highest demand, when people are most likely to need rides, and what problems customers experience with existing transportation options. This information would help me make better decisions about pricing, driver availability, and service areas. Although competitors could research Antigonish, they would not immediately have the same experience gained from actually being present in the community. This advantage would become more valuable as Anti-Walk collects more information. 

A third resource would be the information collected through the platform. Over time, Anti-Walk would build a record of popular pickup locations, busy periods, common routes, and customer demand. This data would help identify where additional drivers are needed and how to make the service more efficient. It would be valuable because it supports better business decisions. A competitor could collect similar information, but they would have to build their own customer base and gather enough data first.

A competitor with a large budget could copy the website, offer lower prices, and spend money recruiting drivers. However, they would still need time to develop a reliable driver network, gain local experience, and collect useful customer data. These advantages could take months or years to develop. In my opinion the driver network would become the strongest advantage over time because it would be the hardest to replicate.

## 5. Cloud and operations

Running a real version of Anti-Walk would require multiple cloud services to keep the platform operating smoothly. Rather than developing and managing everything myself, I would have to rely on existing providers to handle certain functions so I could focus on growing the business; while also saving money by not investing thousands of dollars into hardware and equipment. 

For website hosting and storage, I could use Amazon Web Services (AWS), which offers Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS). This would provide the backend power needed to keep Anti-Walk online. I would also need a managed database to store customer accounts, driver information, and ride records. This would fall under Platform as a Service (PaaS), since the provider manages the underlying technology. Some PaaS companies I could go with are Google Cloud or IBM.

For payments, I could use Stripe, a Software as a Service (SaaS) provider, to process customer payments and driver payouts. I would also use Google Maps Platform for GPS tracking, directions, and estimated arrival times. Google maps platform would also help drivers find the fastest routes, reducing travel time and making the service more convenient for both drivers and customers

## 6. Risks and ethics

One of the biggest risks with Anti-Walk would be customer safety. Since customers would be getting into vehicles with drivers they may not know, Drivers would be required to go through an approval process. This would include verifying their identity, driver's licence, driving history, and a criminal background check.

Another risk would be the personal information collected through the app. Anti-Walk would store customer names, phone numbers, pickup and drop-off locations, payment details, and ride history. If this information was accessed without permission, it could put customers' privacy and safety at risk. I would limit access to this information by only letting a select few, if not just me, have permission to access the information. Additionally, I would only use secure payment providers.

A third risk would be not having enough reliable drivers available. If drivers frequently cancel rides or fail to show up, customers could lose trust in Anti-Walk. I would build strong relationships with drivers, monitor customer feedback, and remove drivers who repeatedly provide unreliable service.
